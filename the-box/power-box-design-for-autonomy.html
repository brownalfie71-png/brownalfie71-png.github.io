Power Box: Design for Autonomy

29 Oct 2026 | 2 min read

Introduction to the Power Box

Good afternoon! Today we will be looking at the Power Box, created by Fam Studio in collaboration with the Open Energy Labs. This clever device helps shrink the skills gap in sub-saharan Africa by allowing people to develop the abilities they need to fix their own electronics.

Around 600 million people live without grid electricity, and roughly 250 million solar products are lying broken around their homes. Off grid solar is unable to be repaired by the user as they do not have the skills required to maintain their electronics.

The power box is the main component in the Energy Makers Academy kit, which includes a small solar panel, tools and over 150 components as well as a phone app to guide them through the process. The hardware is made to be taken apart as the first step which reinforces repair in the user's mind. This is supported by their large array of testing in Kenya and Rwanda where they encourage ‘intergenerational learning’ so that everyone can become self-sufficient.

The kit is a big investment for the youth they want to target, with there being just under 300 graduates of the program so far. This suggests this is another circular product aimed at the few rather than those who truly need it.

There are lessons from this project I will be taking and applying to my own work. I am developing a solution to e-waste and the right to repair but under the scope of the gaming industry, more specifically gaming controllers. This has opened my eyes up to the possible directions I could take my project in and how teaching can be a better method of circularity than creating a whole new product.

Open Energy Labs is now taking reservations offering financing and a university backed certificate, with Strathmore University as a partner. If they can bring costs down, this agency by design model could spread well past Kenya, Zambia and Rwanda.
